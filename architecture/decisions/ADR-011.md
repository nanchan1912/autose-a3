# ADR-011: Reconsider Opus for the reviewer; Sonnet is the candidate replacement

Status: Proposed (would supersede ADR-006 once confirmed on harder seeded defects)
Date: 2026-11-24
Decision: D6 (Models), D8 (Reviewer independence)

## Context

ADR-006 put the reviewer on Opus 5.5 because reviewing needs careful reasoning, and a different model
should also support reviewer independence. Experiment E1 tested this on the five seeded PRs: the
default (Opus), `E1-same-model` (Sonnet 5.5) and `E1-sees-rationale` (Opus that sees the author's PR
description) all reached 5/5 correct verdicts (3/3 planted defects caught, 0/2 false alarms). Sonnet
cost $0.23 for the five reviews, Opus $0.58.

## Decision

The evidence does not support the main reason given in ADR-006: in E1 the stronger, different model
gave no measurable advantage. Sonnet 5.5 is the candidate reviewer model. Because all three variants
hit the ceiling (the seeded defects were probably too easy, n = 1 per cell), the switch is not made yet:
it is made, and ADR-006 marked Superseded, only if Sonnet also matches Opus on a set of harder,
more subtle seeded defects.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep Opus without further testing.** Rejected: E1 gives no evidence for its extra cost.
- **Switch to Sonnet now.** Rejected for now: a ceiling effect on five easy PRs cannot show that Sonnet
  is as good on subtle defects, and the reviewer is the last check before a person.

## Consequences

- The reviewer stays on Opus until the harder experiment is run; ADR-006 stays Accepted until then.
- If confirmed, review cost drops by about 60% and the "different model" layer of reviewer independence
  (ADR-008) is lost; the other layers (own job, no artifacts, no push, evidence required) remain.
